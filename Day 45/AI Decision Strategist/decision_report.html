The Case For Each Option

Option A — B.Tech Computer Science

Strongest case: Builds hard technical skills that stay valuable regardless of what you do later — including modernizing the family business with tech if you go that route.

Hidden upside: A tech degree is a credential that's hard to pick up casually later. Business operations, by contrast, can often be learned on the job inside the family business itself.

Biggest weakness: If your real interest and opportunity is running the business, four years in CS may delay that transition rather than support it.

Best if you value: keeping technical career options open, even at the cost of a longer detour from the family business.

Option B — BBA

Strongest case: Directly prepares you for the path your gut is already pointing to — running or supporting the family business.

Hidden upside: A business degree gives you the language and frameworks to work with your family sooner, and it's often easier to layer tech skills on top of business knowledge (via courses, certifications, or hiring technical people) than the reverse.

Biggest weakness: If the family business path doesn't work out or doesn't materialize the way you expect, you may have narrower options than a technical degree would've given you.

Best if you value: aligning your education with what you're already leaning toward, and getting into the family business sooner.
